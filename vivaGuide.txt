Lab 1: Autoencoder and VAE on MNIST
What happens. Part 1 builds a plain autoencoder (784 → 256 → 64 → 256 → 784), trains it
with MSE, and shows originals vs reconstructions, latent vectors, and an interpolation
between two digits (3 → 8). Part 2 builds a VAE (latent dim 20) that learns a distribution
instead of a point, and generates brand-new digits by sampling random noise.
How. The VAE encoder outputs mu and logvar. We sample z = mu + sigma * eps (eps ~
N(0,1)); this is the reparameterization trick, so gradients can flow through the sampling.
Loss = BCE reconstruction + KL divergence to N(0,I). After training, throw the encoder
away: sample z ~ N(0,I) and pass it through the decoder.
Viva.
Q: Why can't a plain autoencoder generate new images? A: Its latent space has holes
and no known distribution to sample from, so random points decode to garbage.
Q: What is the reparameterization trick? A: Write z = mu + sigma * eps so the
randomness sits in eps, and mu and sigma stay differentiable.
Q: Write the VAE loss. A: Reconstruction loss + KL(q(z|x) || N(0,I)). KL has the closed
form -0.5 * sum(1 + logvar - mu² - exp(logvar)).
Q: Why predict logvar, not variance? A: The network can output any real number and
exp(logvar) is always positive. It is also numerically stable.
Q: What happens if the KL term is dropped? If the reconstruction term is dropped?
A: Without KL it becomes a plain AE with a gappy latent space. Without reconstruction
every input collapses to N(0,I) and the latent code is useless (posterior collapse).
Q: Why are VAE images blurry? A: Pixel-wise losses average over possibilities, and the
KL term trades sharpness for a smooth latent space.

Q: What is ELBO? A: The evidence lower bound on log-likelihood. Maximising ELBO
equals minimising the VAE loss.
Q: Why does your KL loss go up early in training? A: The encoder first uses the latent
space freely to reconstruct, then the KL pull stabilises it.
Q: Name a VAE variant. A: Beta-VAE (weights KL for disentangling), Conditional VAE
(generate a chosen digit), ConvVAE (sharper images).
Lab 2: GAN on MNIST
What happens. A Generator (noise 100 → 256 → 512 → 1024 → 784, Tanh) and a
Discriminator (784 → 1024 → 512 → 256 → 1, Sigmoid) play a game. 50 epochs, Adam lr
0.0002, betas (0.5, 0.999). You plotted G/D loss, image grids at epochs 1 to 50, and a proxy
FID (92.67) and proxy IS (7.48).
How. Each batch: (a) train D on real images (label 1) and fake images (label 0); (b) train G
so D says "real" for fakes (label 1). Images are normalised to [-1,1] to match Tanh. Loss is
BCE.
Viva.
Q: Write the GAN objective. A: min_G max_D E[log D(x)] + E[log(1 - D(G(z)))].
Q: What is the non-saturating generator loss? A: G minimises -log D(G(z)) instead of
log(1 - D(G(z))), which gives stronger gradients early.
Q: What is mode collapse? A: G produces only a few kinds of outputs (e.g., only a few
digits) because they fool D.
Q: What is D loss at equilibrium? A: About log 4 = 1.386 (total over real and fake). With
the averaged loss in your code, about 0.693.
Q: Why do GAN losses oscillate? A: Two networks chase a moving target, so a falling
loss does not mean better images. Look at samples.
Q: Why LeakyReLU, Dropout in D, Tanh in G, beta1 = 0.5? A: LeakyReLU avoids dead
neurons, Dropout slows D, Tanh matches [-1,1] pixels, beta1 = 0.5 stabilises adversarial
training.
Q: What are IS and FID? A: IS rewards confident and diverse classifications (higher is
better). FID compares Gaussian fits of real vs fake features (lower is better). FID is
more reliable.
Q: Why a proxy FID for MNIST? A: Real FID uses ImageNet Inception features, which
do not suit 28x28 grayscale digits.

Lab 3: Fine-tuning GPT-2 / DistilGPT2
What happens. A pretrained DistilGPT2 is fine-tuned on a small AI-topic text corpus with
causal language modelling. You then generate text from prompts and study temperature,
top-k, top-p, and plot loss/perplexity vs epochs.
How. Tokenization turns text into token IDs. Embedding turns IDs into vectors. The model
predicts the next token; loss is cross-entropy; perplexity = exp(loss).
Viva.
Q: Tokenization vs embedding? A: Tokenization maps text to integer IDs. Embedding
maps those IDs to dense vectors the model can learn.
Q: What is causal language modelling? A: Predict the next token using only previous
tokens (masked attention).
Q: What is transfer learning / fine-tuning? A: Start from pretrained weights and train a
little on your own data so it picks up your domain style.
Q: Temperature, top-k, top-p? A: Temperature rescales logits (low = safe, high =
random). Top-k keeps the k best tokens. Top-p keeps the smallest set whose
probability adds to p.
Q: What is perplexity? A: exp(average cross-entropy). It is the model's effective
number of choices per token. Lower is better.
Q: Why set pad_token = eos_token? A: GPT-2 has no pad token by default.
Q: Why can fine-tuning on a tiny corpus overfit? A: The model memorises. Perplexity
on training data drops but quality does not generalise.

Lab 5: Autoencoder-based Face Swapping (educational)
What happens. You trained a shared encoder and two decoders (A and B) on 64x64
Olivetti faces using NumPy only, with augmentation. Swap = encode A, decode with B.
Reported reconstruction MSE: A 0.00315, B 0.00513.
How. The shared encoder keeps pose and expression (identity-neutral). Each decoder
learns one person's appearance. Crossing the wires gives B's face with A's pose. Manual
backprop with Adam, tanh latent, sigmoid output.
Viva.
Q: Why one encoder and two decoders? A: The shared encoder cannot store person
specific detail, so it keeps general structure; decoders store identity.
Q: Why augmentation? A: Only 10 photos per person; shifts, flips and brightness make
more training data without changing identity.
Q: Why are swaps blurry? A: Small data, a simple MLP, and MSE loss.
Q: Ethical concerns of deepfakes? A: Misinformation, consent, fraud. Mention
detection and watermarking.
Q: How do real deepfake systems improve it? A: Convolutional encoders, GANs, face
alignment and blending.

Lab 6: AI Music with OpenAI Jukebox
What happens. Jukebox (1b_lyrics) generated 18 s of Jazz (genre = Jazz, artist unknown,
no lyrics) in about 8.6 minutes, saved as WAV. Integrity checks passed. Waveform and
spectrogram were plotted; a second genre was compared.
How. A 3-level VQ-VAE compresses raw audio to discrete codes. A Transformer prior
generates top-level codes from the genre/artist/lyrics labels; upsamplers add detail; the
decoder makes audio. You decoded from top-level codes only, so it sounds rough.
Viva.
Q: Why is raw audio hard to generate? A: 44,100 samples per second means huge
sequences.
Q: What is a VQ-VAE? A: A VAE with a discrete codebook latent space.
Q: Why three levels? A: Top level captures long-range structure, bottom level captures
fine timbre.
Q: Conditioning in Jukebox? A: Genre, artist and lyrics only. No direct mood or tempo
control. Mood is approximated by choosing the genre.
Q: Waveform vs spectrogram? A: Waveform is amplitude vs time. Spectrogram is
frequency content vs time (STFT).
Q: How do you know the WAV is valid? A: It loads, duration matches, max amplitude
above zero, no NaN/Inf.
Q: Why was it slow? A: Autoregressive sampling over thousands of tokens in windows.
Q: Newer alternatives? A: MusicGen, MusicLM, Suno.
Lab 7: CycleGAN (Horse to Zebra)
What happens. Two generators (G_AB, G_BA) and two 70x70 PatchGAN discriminators
were trained on unpaired horse/zebra images (256x256, batch 4, 10 epochs, lr 2e-4, betas
(0.5, 0.999), lambda_cycle = 10, identity 5). Final losses: cycle 0.2694, identity 0.2621.
How. Total loss = adversarial (LSGAN/MSE) + 10 * cycle loss (L1 of F(G(x)) vs x) + identity
loss. Image pool of 50, linear LR decay, AMP.
Viva.
Q: Why does it work without paired images? A: Cycle consistency forces the
translation to keep the content so it can be reversed.

Lab 9: Diffusion (DDPM) and Stable Diffusion
What happens. Part 1: DDPM on two moons. Part 2: compared linear vs cosine schedules
for T in {10, 50, 200, 1000}. Part 3: Stable Diffusion v1.5 with prompt, guidance scale and
steps varied.
How. Forward: x_t = sqrt(alpha_bar_t) * x0 + sqrt(1 - alpha_bar_t) * eps. The network
predicts eps; loss is MSE. Reverse sampling starts from pure noise. Stable Diffusion does
this in the VAE latent space with a text-conditioned U-Net. Classifier-free guidance: eps =
eps_uncond + w * (eps_cond - eps_uncond).
Viva.
Q: What does the network predict? A: The noise that was added.
Q: Why does x_t have a closed form? A: Gaussian noise steps combine, so any step
can be sampled directly with alpha_bar_t.
Q: Linear vs cosine? A: Cosine destroys signal more gradually. Linear T=10 failed in
your run because the betas hit the 0.999 clamp.
Q: Why should signal at T be near zero? A: x_T must be pure noise so sampling can
start from N(0,I).
Q: Guidance scale effect? A: Low = muddy and ignores the prompt. 7 to 12 = good.
Very high = oversaturated.
Q: Why fewer steps are faster but worse? A: Fewer denoising iterations means a
rougher image (2 steps blurry, 10 to 20 clean).
Q: Why is Stable Diffusion cheaper? A: Diffusion in a small latent space, not pixels.
Q: Diffusion vs GAN? A: Diffusion trains stably and covers modes but is slow at
sampling. GANs are fast but unstable.
Q: Is lower loss better samples? A: Not necessarily. Your linear loss was lower but the
cosine schedule has a different noise-level mix.

PS1 and PS2: Markov chain text generation
What happens. A table maps each word (or k characters) to the list of things that
followed it in a short typed text. Generation repeatedly picks a random next item.
Viva.
Q: What is a Markov chain? A: The next state depends only on the current state.
Q: How is this like an LLM? A: Both do next-token prediction. An LLM uses a neural
network and long context; this uses a lookup table.
Q: Why does repeated text appear? A: The corpus is tiny, so few next options exist.
Q: Effect of order k? A: Higher k copies the source more closely; lower k is more
random.
Q: Why does picking from a list with duplicates work? A: Frequent followers appear
more often, so the choice follows the real probabilities.
PS3: Bigram model, sampling controls, perplexity
What happens. Count word pairs, turn counts into probabilities, then sample with
temperature, top-k and top-p. Compute perplexity.
Viva.
Q: Effect of temperature? A: Low (0.3) repeats safe words; high (2.0) produces
nonsense.
Q: Difference between top-k and top-p? A: Top-k has a fixed count; top-p adapts to
the model's confidence.
Q: Why add smoothing? A: So unseen pairs are not zero probability (log of zero).
Q: Formula for perplexity? A: exp(-average log probability of the true next word).

PS5, PS6, PS7: Genetic and evolutionary generation
What happens. Start from random pixels (or letters). Fitness counts how many match the
target (cross, or a sentence). The best individuals are kept, mixed (crossover) and flipped
randomly (mutation) until a perfect match. PS6 is a simpler (1+1) evolution: flip one pixel,
keep if not worse.
Viva.
Q: What is the fitness function? A: Number of pixels equal to the target.
Q: Roles of selection, crossover, mutation? A: Selection keeps good candidates,
crossover combines them, mutation adds variety to avoid getting stuck.
Q: What is elitism? A: Always copy the best individual into the next generation.
Q: Why is this "generative"? A: It creates new images from noise through iterative
optimisation, with no training data.
Q: What if the mutation rate is too high or too low? A: Too high is random search; too
low gets stuck in local optima.
Q: How is it different from a neural generator? A: It optimises one image against a
known target, and does not learn a distribution.

Quick Reference
Autoencoder: compress and rebuild. VAE: AE plus a distribution and KL, so it can
generate.
GAN: G vs D, adversarial. CycleGAN: two GANs plus cycle loss for unpaired
translation.
Diffusion: add noise step by step, learn to predict it, reverse from pure noise.
GPT / chatbot: next-token prediction, sampling by temperature, top-k, top-p.
Metrics: Perplexity (language, lower better); IS (higher better); FID (lower better).
Offline ESE tip: know the screenshots' three ideas well: Markov text (random only),
genetic image evolution (numpy + matplotlib), evolutionary 3x3 image (numpy).
